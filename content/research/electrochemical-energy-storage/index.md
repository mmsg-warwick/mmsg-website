Batteries store energy in chemical form and release it as electricity when it is needed, and they are central to electric vehicles and to integrating renewable energy into the grid. We focus on lithium-ion batteries, whose behaviour depends on processes across many scales, from the transport of lithium through porous electrodes to the heat generated in a cell and the side reactions that cause it to degrade.

Our models range from detailed physics-based descriptions to reduced-order models derived systematically from them, which are fast enough for practical use while remaining accurate and interpretable. We use them to understand degradation, to parameterise and design cells, and to support battery management.
